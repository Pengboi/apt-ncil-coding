Section A: Reading - Text Extract

From 'The Midnight Visitor' by adapted for educational use

The old house stood at the end of the lane, its windows dark and empty like sockets in a skull. Sarah approached slowly, her footsteps crunching on the gravel. She had been warned about this place, told to stay away by her grandmother and the other villagers. But something drew her here tonight, something she couldn't explain.

The door creaked open before she touched it, revealing a hallway thick with dust and shadows. A cold breeze passed through, though no windows were open. Sarah hesitated at the threshold, her heart hammering against her ribs. The stories said the house was haunted by its former owner, a woman who had died waiting for her lost love to return.

"Hello?" Sarah called, her voice trembling. The sound echoed back at her, distorted and strange. From somewhere upstairs came a sound like footsteps, slow and deliberate. Sarah swallowed hard, gripping her phone tighter. She should leave, she knew that logically. But curiosity and something else, something that felt almost like destiny, kept her feet rooted to the spot.

Question 1 4 marks

Look at the first paragraph. How does the writer create a sense of mystery and tension?

You should write about specific language choices and their effects.

Question 2 4 marks

What impression do you get of Sarah from the extract? Support your answer with evidence from the text.

Question 3 8 marks

How does the writer use language and structure to build suspense?

Write about the atmosphere, Sarah's reactions, and how the writer keeps the reader engaged.

Section B: Non-Fiction Text

Article: Should Teenagers Have Curfews?

In recent years, the debate about teenage curfews has become increasingly heated. Some parents and politicians argue that strict curfews are necessary for safety and discipline, while others believe they infringe on young people's freedom and independence.

Supporters of curfews point to statistics showing that youth crime rates drop when curfews are enforced. They also argue that teenagers need structure and boundaries to develop into responsible adults. "Young people need guidance, not unlimited freedom," says local councillor Mary Thompson.

However, critics argue that curfews unfairly target young people and don't address the root causes of antisocial behaviour. Teenager Jake Williams, 16, says: "We're not all criminals. Most of us just want to hang out with friends. A blanket curfew punishes everyone for the actions of a few."

Question 4 4 marks

According to the article, what are the main arguments FOR teenage curfews?

Question 5 6 marks

How does the writer present different viewpoints about curfews? Consider the structure and language used.

Question 6 12 marks

Write a letter to your local newspaper giving your views on teenage curfews. You can use ideas from the article, but you should also include your own opinions and experiences.

Remember to use appropriate register and include persuasive techniques.

Question 7 12 marks

Write the opening of a story about someone who breaks the rules.

Focus on creating character and atmosphere through your description and dialogue.

Award marks for analysis of:

  • Metaphor: "windows dark and empty like sockets in a skull" - creates horror/gothic atmosphere ✓

  • Sensory details: "footsteps crunching on the gravel" - creates sound imagery ✓
