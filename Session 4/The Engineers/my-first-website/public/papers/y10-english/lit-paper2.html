An Inspector Calls - J.B. Priestley
From Act 3: The Inspector's final speech

INSPECTOR: But just remember this. One Eva Smith has gone—but there are millions and millions and millions of Eva Smiths and John Smiths still left with us, with their lives, their hopes and fears, their suffering and chance of happiness, all intertwined with our lives, and what we think and say and do. We don't live alone. We are members of one body. We are responsible for each other. And I tell you that the time will soon come when, if men will not learn that lesson, then they will be taught it in fire and blood and anguish. Good night.

[The INSPECTOR walks straight out, leaving them staring, subdued and wondering]

Section B: Poetry (32 marks)

Answer one question from this section on your chosen cluster.

Cluster: Power and Conflict

London
William Blake

I wander thro' each charter'd street,
Near where the charter'd Thames does flow.
And mark in every face I meet
Marks of weakness, marks of woe.

In every cry of every Man,
In every Infant's cry of fear,
In every voice: in every ban,
The mind-forg'd manacles I hear.

How the Chimney-sweeper's cry
Every blackning Church appalls,
And the hapless Soldier's sigh
Runs in blood down Palace walls.

But most thro' midnight streets I hear
How the youthful Harlot's curse
Blasts the new-born Infant's tear,
And blights with plagues the Marriage hearse.

Section C: Unseen Poetry (32 marks)

Read the poem below and answer the questions that follow.

The Jetty
By a contemporary poet

Weathered planks lean into the lake,
each one a diary of summers—
initials carved, then blurred
by decades of water and wind.

I walk to where the wood grows soft,
spongy with rot, yet somehow still
bearing the weight of those who came
to dive into the blue unknown.

Below, the fish have grown lazy, fat on breadcrumbs and legends
of children who once believed
they could hold their breath forever.

Now the jetty holds only me,
my shadow longer than the planks,
and somewhere a radio plays
the same song from a different year.

Compare this poem with 'The Jetty':

Old Photographs
By another contemporary poet

The album opens like a door
to rooms we can no longer enter.
Here: my mother young and laughing,
not yet knowing what she'd lose.

The colours fade year by year,
but the smiles remain stubborn,
refusing to admit that everyone
in this picture is already gone.

I am the curator of these ghosts,
turning pages like a prayer,
trying to remember the sound
of a voice the camera never caught.
